The fifth-century Armenian historian Lazar P'arpec'i includes two groups of captives in his History of the Armenians, one composed primarily of nobles who were initially sent to Gorgan (Latin. Hyrcania, the region north of the Alborz watershed and bordering the Caspian Sea to the south-east) and the other of noble children held in Khuzestan, with both groups also containing Christian priests. Lazar is a particularly valuable source for these captives, in part because he knew several of them personally. Most notably, his history was sponsored by Vahan Mamikonean – the eldest of three brothers who are some of the only identifiable captives of Khuzestan and who led his own rebellion against the Sasanians from AD 482–4. Lazar had been Vahan's childhood playmate after the noble's release, and was appointed as abbot of the monastery of Valarsapat (modern Ejmiacin) by Vahan c. 493 AD, writing to his patron to defend his conduct after the monks had him removed.
